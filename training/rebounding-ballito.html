Rebounding
In Ballito.

Rebounding, also called bounce fitness or mini-trampoline fitness, is exercise on a mini-trampoline. You bounce, jog, jump and move to music, and the springy mat turns simple moves into a workout for your heart, legs, core and balance, without the hard landings of running on the road.

How hard you work depends on how high and how fast you bounce, so the same class can be gentle or tough. Thalia shows you how to make every move easier or harder, so you can work at your own level. That makes it one of the most welcoming low-impact exercise options in Ballito, and a fun group fitness class to do with friends.
  • Cardio fitness for your heart and lungs
  • Leg, glute and core strength
  • Balance, coordination and posture
  • Gentler on the joints than running
  • A fun, upbeat one-hour class set to music
  • Your rebounder is provided

Rebounding Benefits

The Physical Benefits of
Rebounding.

Rebounding looks like play, but research shows it is a serious workout. Here is what the studies have found.

Heart & Lungs
A Real Cardio Workout
In a study sponsored by the American Council on Exercise, a 19-minute mini-trampoline routine kept heart rates at about 79% of maximum, which meets the guidelines for improving cardio fitness.1
Energy
Burns Energy Like a Run
The same study measured an average of about 11 calories a minute for men and 8 for women. In a separate study of women, one session of about 46 minutes burned around 317 calories.1,2
Joints
Gentler Landings
The mat gives under you and absorbs part of each landing, so you get your heart rate up with less pounding through the knees, hips and back than running on a hard surface.3
Balance
Balance & Coordination
Every bounce is a small balance challenge. In a 12-week trial in women aged 56 to 83, twice-weekly mini-trampoline training improved one-leg balance by about a third.4
Strength
Stronger Legs & Core
Jumping, landing and controlling the bounce works the legs, glutes and core. In the same trial, chair-stand strength improved by 36%.4
Fun
Feels Easier Than It Is
People in the ACE study rated the workout as easier than their heart rates showed it to be, which the researchers said helps people stick with exercise.1
Feel Good

The Mental Benefits of
Rebounding.

How you feel after a class matters as much as what it does for your body.

Mood
A Natural Mood Lift
Aerobic exercise is one of the most effective kinds of exercise for easing symptoms of depression and anxiety, according to a large 2026 review of 63 randomised trials.5
Stress
Bounce the Stress Out
Rhythmic movement to music gives your mind a break from the day. An hour that is just for you, where you can't think about your to-do list while you bounce.
Confidence
More Confidence on Your Feet
In the 12-week trial, fear of falling dropped by 15% as balance and strength improved, a real boost to confidence in everyday life.4
Together
The Energy of a Group
The same review found supervised and group exercise worked best for easing depression. Training with others brings support, accountability and a lot of laughter.5
Habit
Fun You'll Keep Coming Back To
Because rebounding feels like play, it's easier to keep doing week after week, and consistency is what changes your fitness and how you feel.
Mindset
Coached for Body and Mind
Thalia is also an NLP practitioner and life coach, so classes are about feeling good in yourself, not just about the workout.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked
Questions.

What is rebounding?
Rebounding is exercise on a mini-trampoline (a rebounder). You bounce, jog, jump and move in time to music, and the springy mat turns simple moves into a full-body cardio, strength and balance workout.
Where can I find rebounding classes near me?
If you live in Ballito, Salt Rock, Shakaskraal, Zimbali, Umhlali or elsewhere on the KZN North Coast, our rebounding classes are at Maddog Performance Institute, 22 Sandra Road, Balvista Centre, Ballito, on Wednesdays at 9am.
What are the benefits of rebounding?
Studies show rebounding raises your heart rate enough to count as a real cardio workout while feeling easier than it is, spreads landing forces more evenly than running, and improved balance and leg strength in older women. Like other aerobic exercise, it can also help your mood. See the research section on this page for the details and sources.
Is rebounding a good group fitness class for women?
Yes. Rebounding suits women of every age and fitness level, because you choose how high and how fast you bounce. Several of the studies on this page were done with women, including women over 55. It is a fun, sociable group class set to music.
Do I need to be fit to do a rebounding class?
No. How hard you work depends on how high and how fast you bounce, so the same class can be gentle for a beginner and tough for someone fitter. Thalia shows you how to make each move easier or harder.
When are the rebounding classes?
Rebounding runs on Wednesdays at 9am at Maddog Performance Institute, 22 Sandra Road, Balvista Centre, Ballito. Each class is one hour, and bookings are essential.
Do I need my own rebounder?
No. A rebounder (mini-trampoline) is provided for you in every class.
Is my first rebounding class free?
Yes. Your first class is a free trial class. Booking is still essential, so send us a WhatsApp to book your spot.
How much does rebounding cost at Maddog?
Your first class is a free trial. After that, a single class is R150. A 4-class package is R500. Packages must be used within 30 days of purchase.
How do I book a rebounding class?
Bookings are essential, because space on the trampolines is limited. Send us a WhatsApp on 063 442 1690 to book your spot.
Is rebounding a low-impact exercise?
Yes. The trampoline mat gives under you and absorbs part of each landing, so many people find it gentler on the joints than running on a hard surface, while still getting their heart rate up.
Is rebounding safe if I'm pregnant or have a pelvic floor, back or joint problem?
Check with your doctor or physiotherapist first, and tell your instructor before the class. Bouncing puts load on the pelvic floor, so anyone who is pregnant, has recently given birth, or has bladder leaks, back, knee or balance problems should get medical advice and may need gentler options.
Who teaches rebounding at Maddog?
Thalia De Beer of Bounce Ballito Rebound Fitness. She is a qualified rebounding instructor who has worked in fitness since 2004, with qualifications including Exercise Science and Personal Training (Bodyline SA), ISSA Certified Personal Trainer, CrossFit Level 2 and nutrition coaching, plus NLP and life coaching.
What should I wear?
Comfortable training clothes you can move freely in, and a supportive sports bra if you wear one. Bring water and a towel.
