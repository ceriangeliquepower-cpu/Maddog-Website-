What Is Sports Physiotherapy — and Why Does It Matter?

Sports physiotherapy is a specialised branch of physiotherapy focused on the prevention, diagnosis, treatment, and rehabilitation of sports and exercise-related injuries. Unlike general physiotherapy, sports physio is built around the specific demands that physical performance places on the human body — understanding how muscles, joints, tendons, and the nervous system interact under load, stress, and repeated movement.

A great sports physiotherapist does not just treat injuries after they happen. The best practitioners prevent them from happening in the first place — through preseason planning, load management, movement screening, and building the kind of injury resilience that keeps athletes performing at their best for longer.

Robin's Masters research at UCT sits at the centre of one of the more urgent problems in South African youth sport. Concussion in adolescent rugby players is a well-documented and growing public health concern — younger players carry a higher risk, and the resulting symptoms can affect a young person's social, emotional and psychological wellbeing for years after the initial knock.

Despite this, research consistently shows a knowledge gap at coach and community level around identifying, managing and preventing concussion in young players. When Should You See a Sports Physiotherapist?

The most common mistake people make is waiting too long. A niggle ignored for three weeks often becomes a three-month setback. Here are the clearest signals that it is time to book:

  • Pain that persists beyond 48–72 hours after exercise
  • Pain that changes the way you move or causes you to compensate
  • Recurring injury in the same area — particularly hamstrings, ankle, or shoulder
  • Swelling, bruising, or significant loss of range of motion after an acute incident
  • Chronic tension or stiffness that does not respond to stretching or rest
  • A child or adolescent athlete who complains of pain — especially in growing areas
  • Concussion symptoms: headache, dizziness, cognitive fog, emotional changes after head contact
